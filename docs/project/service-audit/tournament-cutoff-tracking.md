# Tournament cutoff tracking

The simulation keeps one process-local tracker per scene and keys registrations by match, generation, start, deadline and roster. Registrations made at or after start or with missing residents are unavailable. The production 1x path polls through the deadline before freezing; no-pending work may yield alive/destroyed evidence. If the only in-flight work is remote repair whose original `at <= cutoff`, a bounded scene fence waits for the matching result, then polls again at the cutoff before freezing. Unclassified or unsupported work, timeout, queue overflow, and unsafe cross-scene changes produce unavailable rather than a winner. Unclassified mutations also abort the fence. The client-facing host consumer must validate the returned identity and treat pending, unavailable, errors, and unknown evidence as non-final.

Docking records departure. Empty public scenes retaining match tracking or release tombstones survive the regular cleanup, failed enter, failed jump arrival, and abyssal trace cleanup paths. Tombstones are process-local, retained without eviction and capped at 4,096 per scene; after process restart the simulation cannot reconstruct past evidence. At capacity, new tombstones fail closed. Ordinary untracked scene cleanup remains unchanged.

Focused validation: dock/reentry tracker retention and failed-enter retention both failed before their fixes and passed after; ordinary docking and tombstone-capacity controls passed. The scene tracker release test verifies duplicate release and late registration fencing. A direct Scene damage/destroy test verifies destroyed evidence at the cutoff and an independent test verifies later destruction cannot rewrite an already frozen alive result; these are not network weapon-kill tests. Sim tests cover wrong/correct/duplicate repair ACK, exact cutoff, timeout, overflow, release, cross-scene move and inbound gate jump, and other public/private scene progress. The real v88 Host + simulator + PostgreSQL test `AbyssalHostFlowTests.Tournament_cutoff_waits_for_actual_host_remote_repair_result` passed 1/1 with no skips: two live pilots, fitted and online module, real skill and target-lock checks, and the normal Host remote-assistance policy produced the actual Host-calculated acknowledgment. Holding that acknowledgment kept the expired cutoff pending; releasing it yielded Ready with the full scene/match/generation/time and pilot/ship identity, repaired armor, and replaying the same acknowledgment did not repair twice. Initial armor damage was seeded as a PostgreSQL ship condition, not produced by combat; this is not a network combat-damage test.

Known limits: unsupported concurrent fuel, reload, drone, structure-authorization or due bomb work remains unavailable. Unknown mutations abort the fence. At the same deadline, an abort in one fenced scene can make all affected scenes unavailable. A late reconnect or process restart cannot recreate evidence. Client GUI behavior has not been tested.
