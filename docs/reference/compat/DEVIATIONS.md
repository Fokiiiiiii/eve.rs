# Deviations from the reference server

The reference (EVE.js) is matched by default (`docs/design/adr/0001-external-compatibility-only.md`).
A deliberate difference goes here, with the evidence that the client cannot tell or that it
is better off, and how it is tested. A behaviour is either:

- **contract**: the client can observe it; eve.rs matches the reference;
- **tolerated**: the client can observe it but accepts a better behaviour; eve.rs may differ,
  and the difference is listed below;
- **internal**: the client cannot observe it; eve.rs is free.

Shape: the same fields, the same order (newest first), the same visibility (a pilot's entry after 10 minutes, `miningCharacterLedgerDelayMs`) and the same 90-day retention. Differences: (a) the numbers are `PyLong` where the game store returned `PyInt` (the client reads them as integers; the marshal type differs); (b) an observer is listed when its first visible time has come and its newest entry is within the retention, which is exact when its entries are all visible or all retained, and approximate when a visible-but-expired entry and a retained-but-hidden entry sit together; (c) the observer names are sorted by the database collation, not the culture's; (d) the game store's rows are copied at each start (adding only the entries that are not there), a shortcut that reads the whole table, and the rows stay there as an archive. Evidence: `MiningLedgerTests` (replay, rollback, visibility, prune, import), `MiningLedgerServiceTests` (the wire fields, order, roles, empty shapes). Status: tolerated; the observer path has no writer, because a ship's laser has no observer structure.
- **Gas clouds sit beside belt rocks.** CCP keeps harvestable gas clouds in gas sites found by scanning, and EveJS places them through its mining-resource
  sites (`miningResourceSiteService.js`). Here a published cloud is seeded beside each belt's rock on a pilot's arrival, when no live cloud is in the belt.
  Site generation is not built.
- **Yield of a cloud.** The SDE material of the cloud (`typeMaterials`), else the compressed gas it is named for (`Compressed <name>`, group Compressed Gas).
- **Quantity of a rock and of a cloud.** 1,000 units each, a fixed shortcut. EveJS estimates a rock's quantity from its radius and a template
  (`miningRuntimeState.js`, `estimateOriginalQuantity`). The source of per-belt contents is decision D8.
- **Crystal volatility is rolled by the host.** EveJS rolls it in the process (`applyCrystalVolatility`). The simulation does not carry the roll, so no wire field
  is added for it. The host draws it with `SpaceContainers.VolatilityRoll`.
- **Crystal damage** is kept as the crystal item's custom info, and is not announced to the client (EveJS `notifyMiningChargeDamageChange`). A crystal at full
  damage is removed (`applyCrystalVolatility`).
- **Crystal consumption.** None per cycle, as in EveJS (`executeMiningCycle` takes no charge per cycle).
- **Crystal yield.** EveJS reads the crystal's yield multiplier (attribute 782) in its module snapshot and does not read the waste bonuses (attributes
  3159 and 3160, `miningDogma.js`, `buildMiningModuleSnapshot`). eve.rs checks the crystal's type list at activation and does not apply the multiplier
  directly. Whether the multiplier reaches the yield through dogma is not checked in this code path.
- **Passive lock.** The lock stays on the target after the targeter stops. EveJS's deactivation path for `passiveTargetingEffect` was not read.
- **Auto-target at a full lock list.** The sweep passes over the ships it cannot lock, silently. A refusal the sweep did not ask for is not sent.
- **Mining ledger.** One entry per granted cycle. The entry id is the event id, which keeps a replayed event from writing twice. A character's entry is
  visible 10 minutes after its cycle (EveJS `miningCharacterLedgerDelayMs`) and kept for 90 days. Observer rows are written as described in the ledger-table entry above.
- **Yield destination.** The general cargo hold. EveJS prefers the mining hold (`resolveDestinationFlagForPlayer`).
- **Client text.** `TARGET_INVALID_FOR_MODULE` and `CHARGE_NOT_COMPATIBLE` have no dedicated text in EveJS's switch, so the client shows the default
  "Failed to activate" text. The salvage notices use EveJS's names (`SalvagingFailure`, `CustomNotify`); their text in the client is not checked here.

## Tractor beam (protocol 30)

- **A pulled wreck is a ball that follows its puller (D6).** EVE.js `applyTractorActivationCommand` sends `SetBallFree`, `SetBallMass` (10), `SetMaxSpeed`, `SetSpeedFraction` (1) and `FollowBall` to any ball it pulls, wreck or can alike. The client's tactical display reads a ball's `followId` whatever the ball's kind (eve-logic `tacticalNavigation/actionDisplay.py`, `_GetMode`). eve.rs sends the same presentation (`tractor.rs`), and the follow step is generalised to any body (`motion::Kinematics`), not a new motion kind.
- **Inertia of the pulled wreck stays at the ball default (1.0).** EVE.js's beam does not set it; eve.rs's `BallEntity` default is 1.0 (`ball.rs`).
- **Thrust of a light body (a fix to the motion model; ships unchanged).** The thrust is the one whose equilibrium is the speed (`speed * friction / inertial mass`). The old `agility_seconds` floor of 0.05 s held a 10 kg body to about 0.1 m/s. Ships sit far above the floor, so their motion is unchanged (the ship motion tests pass).
- **Hold distance 500 m, the beam's constant (EVE.js `TRACTOR_FOLLOW_RANGE_METERS`).** The speed is the module's `maxTractorVelocity` (attribute 1045).
- **Release.** EVE.js `stopTractorTarget` restores the ball's top speed, zeroes the speed fraction and velocity, and sends `Stop`; it leaves the mass at 10. eve.rs does the same, restoring the top speed of a ball that lies still (0).
- **Loot rights (D13): checked at activation, not every cycle.** EVE.js checks `hasTractorLootRight` every cycle, through the space loot entitlement (`moduleRuntimeCallbacks.hasLootRightForTarget`). eve.rs checks the wreck's stored rights when the beam is activated (`SpaceContainers.MayPullAsync`, the rule of `MayTakeFromAsync`: abandoned, the pilot's own, or its corporation's). The rights change only by abandonment during a pull, which widens them. Choice: the stored snapshot, as the salvage credit uses it.
- **Two pulls on one wreck.** The later beam takes the wreck over; the earlier beam's end leaves it alone. EVE.js does not state this case; this is the simplest rule that keeps one follow target per ball.
- **A puller that leaves.** The pull is let go at the next settlement (`Scene::release_orphaned_pulls`). EVE.js ends the beam with the ship's removal.
- **Corpses are not tractorable.** EVE.js gives a corpse its own runtime kind (`getRuntimeInventoryEntityKind`: the biomass group, scooped to the cargo hold), and `TRACTORABLE_ENTITY_KINDS` names only wreck and container. eve.rs refuses a corpse as not there (`TARGET_NOT_FOUND`).
- **Mobile Tractor Units (group 1250)** are not ship modules and their runtime (EVE.js `mobileTractorUnitRuntime.js`) is not built; the handler is for the ship's `tractorBeamCan`.

## Module rules (track B, 2026-10-09)

Decisions taken where the sources left a choice (EveJS is the trusted reference; CCP docs and the SDE settle the numbers):

- Module rules are enforced in the simulation from the rules the host sends with each effect (`ModuleRules`, flag 32). The host works them out from the type's
  attributes once, in the effect wrapper (`ShipEffect`), so no handler repeats them. Early deactivation (906), the core requirement (3265), the group limit (763)
  and the reactivation delay (669) are the four rules sent. Disallow activate on warp (1245) stays on the host (`ModuleRestrictions.CheckWarp`), as on main.
- Locks from running modules (warp 104, docking 2354, activation block 1349) are read from the running effect's `on_changes`, which the host already sends,
  so they need no wire change. The Networked Sensor Array's block of the capital micro jump drives comes through its modifier (5700, skill 83464).
- Refusal wording: where EveJS has a text it is used (docking, cloak, industrial core, module blocked as the client's `ModuleIsBlocked`). Where EveJS shows a
  client user error that needs data the refusal does not carry (the reactivation delay with `timeSinceLastStop`, the group limit with `count`), the pilot is
  told in a notice of ours, recorded in TODO section 16.
- Dock refusal: EveJS's `DOCKING_DISALLOWED_BY_MODULE` text is shown. The other silent dock refusals (immobile, doomsday, disrupted) are unchanged.
- Repeat cap (disallowRepeatingActivation 1014): the server's cycle budget is one (EveJS `moduleCycleSchedule.js`). The repeat the client is told for the
  effect's FX is also one. EveJS keeps the client's repeat for the FX, so this differs for the FX only; the client's view was not checked.
- Hostility (FP-3, FP-C6): a computer (every percentage on its side) is not hostile; a disruptor is. A guidance disruptor is hostile when its velocity or
  explosion velocity is lowered, or its delay or explosion radius raised, the same signs as the SDE's bonuses. EveJS gives no separate rule for it.
- Spool (wiki gap WG-1): the disintegrator's bonus resets when its target changes and when its effect stops (EveJS `precursorTurrets.js` 157-164 and
  `moduleEffectShutdown.js` 165, 319). EveJS has no reset when the target leaves optimal range, so none is added; the wiki line is not applied.
- The mwd_blocked special case (another ship's scrambler, `Incoming`) stays. It is not an own-module overlay, so the 1349 overlay does not replace it.
- A burnt-out module (its own effect sets its damage to its hit points, the energizer) does not start again (MODULE_INCAPACITATED). The state is kept in the
  simulation and is lost on a host restart; the host's module condition is not updated by it.
- Stall guard (M-1): a module moment that comes back at the same tick for the same module ends that module's effect (Ending::Forced). A parked work cycle
  whose action has gone stops the effect. The budget is 10,000 moments a ship per settle; the rest is due at the next settle.
- ProtocolVersion 32 to 33: one bump for the module rules (flag 32). The fixture generator and `messages.json` carry the frames.

## Launchers A3: festival, countermeasure, F.O.F. (track A leftovers)

- F.O.F. (auto-targeting) missiles: the rule is the EVE University wiki's ("Missiles", auto-targeting missiles): the launcher goes for "the nearest enemy ship
  or drone that has previously fired on you", and no lock is needed. The simulation takes the enemies that the scene's aggressions name as having fired on the
  shooter, the nearest of them within the missile's flight range (speed times explosion delay, from the SDE). The wiki gives a "hard maximum range" with no
  number; EVE Ref lists 200 km for a cruise F.O.F. The SDE number is used (numbers from the SDE, rules from the wiki). Excluded: the shooter's corporation and
  alliance, the shooter's own owner, ships in warp, and ships in undock protection. Standings and war declarations are not consulted (the simulation has none),

  Ties go to the lower ship id. Nothing in reach: nothing fires and nothing is charged.
- Breacher pods (launcher 4807; SDE numbers, wiki rules): the pod's damage-over-time has the SDE values dotDuration 40 s (attribute 5735), a cap of 600 damage
  per tick (5736), and 0.6% of the target's hit points per tick (5737). The wiki gives the tick as one second ("damage per second cap", "percentage of target
  HP damaged per second"), so the tick is one second. The wiki's rules: a pod does nothing unless it reaches its target; it can be killed by a smartbomb before
  it arrives; it hits only player ships and NPCs; one DoT per target at a time, while several ships can carry DoTs from one launcher. The per-tick bonuses (5749, 5750) are
  skill bonuses to the same numbers and are not read yet. The wiki's "75 seconds with max skills" is a skill-modified duration; the base duration is the SDE's

- **Decided: the resistance is read at the cycle's end, not at activation.** EVE.js refuses the start (`TARGET_RESISTS_SCAN`, no cycle spent) when the target's

- **Decided: a ship scan rolls each fitted item.** EVE.js draws once per fitted item when the chance is short of one. The host draws with `Random.Shared` (a seam,

- **Decided: a scan is not an attack.** The SDE gives effects 46 and 47 `isOffensive` false. The simulation starts no weapons timer and no aggression.

  its launch is an aggression though it does no damage. The rate of fire is the launcher's own attribute, as for the other missile launchers.
- A hit: the wiki says "they will not affect their target unless they actually reach and hit" and that a smartbomb can kill a pod before it arrives. The pod flies
  as a missile does (`missile_entity`), so both hold; the simulation takes the pod's damage as zero (its charge has no damage attributes in the SDE).
- Targets: the wiki says a pod "cannot be used against targets such as capsules, structures, stargates etc.", and only player ships and NPCs; the simulation's
  missile targets are ships, so this holds.
- The tick: one second. The wiki says damage "every second"; V24.01's `DOT_TICK_RATE_SEC` is 1 (eve-logic `dotWeapons/common/dotRegistry.py`).
- The damage of a tick: the lower of the percentage of the target's total hit points and the cap, rounded down. The wiki gives "based on the targets total
  hitpoints or a flat cap, whichever is lower", with hit points as shield, armor and hull combined; eve-logic `dotUtil.GetEffectiveDamage` is `int(min(ratio *
  total, cap))`. The total is the three capacities (eve-logic `_GetTotalHp` reads the dogma capacities), not the current points.
- Resistances: the wiki says the effect "completely ignores resistances". The tick goes through the layers with every resonance at one (`Health::apply_unresisted`).
- Stacking: one application works on a ship at a time. The wiki: "the player who has the strongest breacher pod effect will deal damage to the target, and the
  weaker effect stays inert until the stronger one expires". Strongest is the most a tick does at the ship's current total hit points; a tie goes to the one
  already working, then to the one that landed first. An inert application's clock stops until it works. eve-logic differs here: its applications expire on
  absolute time (`expiryTime = added + duration`, `dotPriorityQueue.py`) and pick the highest effective damage with the same tie rule. The wiki is the rule
  (its order is first), so the inert clock is kept; the difference shows only when a weaker effect outlasts the stronger one's run.
- A second pod from the same attacker: the wiki says "a new hit replaces the old effect once it expires". It is read as a queue: it waits, inert, like any other.
  Not a replacement of the attacker's own effect.
- The effect ends when the ship leaves the scene: a jump, a dock or a death (wiki: "until a player leaves the solarsystem or docks up"). It goes on through warp
  and tethers (wiki: "It can continue doing damage while the player is in warp or while the player is tethered"). A restart drops it (the simulation keeps no
  state across a restart, as for missiles in flight).
- A tick that destroys the ship: the killer is the pilot who fired the pod, while that pilot is still in the scene (a choice; the wiki and eve-logic name no
  killer for a DoT death).
- Client-visible: the slim item of the ship carries `dotHpPercentageApplied` = 100 times the tick's damage over the total hit points, told with
  `OnSlimItemChange` when it changes and removed by the plain slim item when the effect ends (eve-logic `dotRegistry.py` `ballpark.UpdateSlimItemField` and
  `DeleteSlimItemField`). A ship that comes into view while a pod's effect runs is not told the field until the next change (a shortcut: the slim item is built
  from the ship, without the effect; upgrade when a ship's slim item reads the applications).
- Not built, a blocker: `OnDotVictimUpdated(targetID, dmgAppsInfo)` (eve-logic `michelle.py`, the attacker's and the target's list of applications). Its

  (`server/src/network/tcp/utils/marshal.js`) carries a set opcode, so the payload cannot be encoded from a source here. Until the set's marshal form is known,

- Per-tick damage messages (`OnDamageMessage`) are not sent for a DoT tick: the sources have no wire shape for it. The target's attributes and damage state go

- Protocol 40 to 41: the missile block (`put_missile`, `read_missile`) ends with a damage-over-time flag, then duration, cap and percentage. A defender's action
